Arsenal

Founded in 1886 by munitions workers in Woolwich as Dial Square, Arsenal Football Club has evolved into one of the most successful and culturally influential institutions in global sport. The club moved to North London's iconic Highbury Stadium in 1913, cementing a fierce local rivalry with Tottenham Hotspur. Arsenal's first golden era emerged in the 1930s under visionary manager Herbert Chapman, who modernized the sport with tactical innovations and led the team to multiple league titles. Decades later, the appointment of Arsène Wenger in 1996 revolutionized English football through sports science and fluid, attacking play. This era was defined by the legendary 2003–04 "Invincibles" season, when Arsenal won the Premier League without losing a single match. After moving to the Emirates Stadium in 2006, the club eventually returned to elite title-contending status under manager Mikel Arteta.

Manchester United

Manchester United was founded in 1878 as Newton Heath LYR Football Club before changing its name in 1902 and moving to Old Trafford in 1910. The club grew into one of the most famous football teams in the world. Manchester United survived the Munich air disaster in 1958, which killed 23 people, including eight players. Manager Matt Busby rebuilt the team, and Manchester United became the first English club to win the European Cup in 1968. Sir Alex Ferguson led the club from 1986 to 2013, creating the most successful period in the club's history. They won 13 Premier League titles and a historic continental treble in 1999. The club has won 20 English league titles.

Manchester City

Manchester City spent decades moving between different tiers of English football, claiming early honors such as the FA Cup in 1904 and league titles. A major turning point arrived in 2008 when the Abu Dhabi United Group took ownership. This investment transformed the club into a global powerhouse. Guided by manager Pep Guardiola and a generation of outstanding players, City achieved a historic treble in 2023 and became the first men's team in English football history to win four consecutive top-flight league titles.

Liverpool

Liverpool Football Club was founded in 1892 following a dispute between the owner of Anfield and the committee of Everton. Liverpool quickly established itself as one of England's most successful football clubs. The club enjoyed major periods of success during the 1970s and 1980s, winning numerous league titles and European trophies. Under managers Bill Shankly and Bob Paisley, Liverpool developed a powerful footballing tradition. In the modern era, Jürgen Klopp guided the club to the Premier League title in 2020 and the UEFA Champions League in 2019, restoring Liverpool to the top of European football.

🔵

Chelsea

Chelsea Football Club was founded in 1905 at The Rising Sun pub by businessman Gus Mears, establishing its permanent home at Stamford Bridge. The West London club achieved its first major success by winning the Football League championship in 1955, followed by various domestic cup victories during the 1960s and 1970s. Chelsea experienced a major transformation in 2003 when Roman Abramovich purchased the club, ushering in an era of unprecedented financial backing and global prominence. Under José Mourinho and subsequent elite coaches, Chelsea became a dominant force in modern football, winning Premier League titles, two UEFA Champions League trophies in 2012 and 2021, and several FA Cups. In 2022, a consortium led by Todd Boehly and Clearlake Capital acquired the club, beginning a new chapter focused heavily on young and international talent.
